Part 2

Wait, that’s not right

The setup: a cup of vinegar sits on a scale with 5.00 g of baking soda in a paper boat beside it. Total on the scale: 145.0 g. You tip the baking soda in. It fizzes.

Predict first: when the fizzing stops, what will the scale read?

The scale dropped about 2.5 g. Why did the mass go down?

Part 3

Build it first, then count it

Draw the reaction with atoms before you look at any answer. Then count every atom on each side yourself. Pick how you want to start:

Count it yourself. How many atoms of each element did you draw on each side?

Do it with your hands (optional). Use clay, paper circles or candy in two colors. Build 2 H₂ and 1 O₂ on your desk, then pull them apart and make 2 H₂O. Use every piece and don’t add any.
Part 4

Why care?

Your soda goes flat because mass leaves the bottle. The fizz is CO₂ dissolved in the drink. Open it and the gas escapes into the room, so the can really does get lighter. It’s the same reason the scale dropped in Part 2. When bread or pancakes rise, CO₂ from baking soda or yeast puffs them up.
